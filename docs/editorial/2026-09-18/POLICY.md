# Editorial draft policy

This is a proposal for the course at commit `2209428d55d3c47ce405dd81b67ab3d0b991a8ac`.
The live source files are not being edited. Drafts mirror their paths under
`draft/`; the comparison records the exact original passage, proposed wording
and reason for each edit.

## Keep

- The engineering deadpan: social conventions are a badly documented legacy
  protocol, and the documentation failure belongs to the institutions and
  conventions that reproduce it.
- Useful scripts, awkward particulars, fictional worked examples labelled as
  such, and jokes that explain the mechanism.
- The mixed classroom, agency to decline, and masking as a cost rather than a
  virtue or personal failing.
- Bibliographic records, attributed quotations, course identity, dates, marks,
  timings, requirements, links and shared technical vocabulary.
- The settled no-diagnosis clause and the week 10 register break, verbatim.

## Edit

- Remove repeated announcements that a point matters, is deliberate, is
  uncomfortable, or demonstrates the course's own virtue. Keep an explanation
  when it changes what a student should do.
- Let examples and instructions end without a second sentence interpreting
  their significance. Preserve short punchlines when they earn their place.
- Reduce repeated rhetorical patterns across weeks. Do not flatten every
  contrast, fragment, metaphor or three-part list into neutral institutional
  prose: those are tools, not evidence of authorship.
- Cut or recast unsupported claims about what most students, pairs or rooms
  will discover. Use open questions where the activity should find out.
- Preserve the strength and limits of research claims. Do not add findings or
  make a source sound more conclusive. Flag questions requiring research
  separately rather than quietly resolving them through style.
- Bring slides and speaker notes into agreement with the edited lecture;
  preserve slide structure, timings and teaching activities.

## Scope and review

Read all authored course text: lectures and their specifications, labs,
readings annotations and verification notes, assessment briefs, staff profiles,
slide text and speaker notes, pages, captions, diagram labels, alt text,
glossary, catalogue and interface strings. Source-code comments, research
working papers and agent/process documentation are context, not student prose.
The comparison includes retained files so unchanged text is accounted for.

This is an editorial judgement, not an AI-authorship detector. No target cut
percentage or phrase blacklist is used. The draft will be checked in an
isolated copy; validation cannot establish the factual accuracy of all the
cited research.
