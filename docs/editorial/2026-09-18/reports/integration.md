# Integrated editorial review

The weekly and shared-copy reports record the first review pass. This report
records the later integration decisions; the exact review records and complete
mirrored drafts are authoritative where those earlier reports leave a question
open.

## Changes made during integration

- **Repair timing, week 5:** distinguish the time to initiate repair from its
  total duration. Keep the specific clarification script without claiming the
  cited timing study proves that it finishes faster.
- **Forgiveness, week 5:** preserve the reported variance comparison and
  coefficients, but stop turning them into a ranking of causes for a particular
  apology. The replacement marginal joke is “The situation belongs in the bug
  report.”
- **Anticipated stigma, week 10:** present routine departmental adjustments as
  a proposal to test, consistent with the revised reading annotation. Remove
  the imagined controlled comparison between two people and the assertion that
  a department's causal effect was established. The self-check now asks for a
  proposal that *might* reduce the cost.
- **Readability, week 11:** preserve the correlation and the group difference.
  Remove the claim that diagnosis had no relationship to ratings or that a
  particular hard-to-read neurotypical target necessarily received identical
  treatment. The self-check and presenter note ask for a bounded reading.
- **Clause count, week 11:** “Draft the rest on Thursday” replaces “Five is
  Thursday's job”. The lab and report still require at least four clauses.
- **ARPANET caption, week 11:** inspection of the image shows repeated machine
  models. The caption now describes different makes on one network; it makes
  no claim that every box differs or that no machine was ever replaced. The
  map's own accuracy disclaimer and the interoperation analogy remain.
- **Timeout glossary entry:** carry over the lecture's telephone-call setting
  and distinguish the observed pattern from decoding any individual pause.
- **Timing trap, week 10:** qualify the predicted listener reaction in the
  lecture, deck and marginal notes; the early-disclosure slide no longer calls
  the option low-stakes for everyone.
- **Journal worked example:** its test establishes a different preferred day,
  not why the person paused. The revised conclusion says what remains
  untested, matching week 4's advice about any particular pause.
- **Assessment and site copy:** retain all submission requirements and useful
  operational detail. Remove statements that the deadline rules demonstrate
  the course's virtue, that a word limit explains AI misuse, or that an access
  arrangement costs every student nothing. The homepage now states that the
  journal begins in week 2.

These are proposals in the editorial package only. The work does not claim a
fresh verification of the papers: it narrows wording to the findings already
recorded in the repository and makes the course's inferences visible.

## Decisions left for a substantive pass

### Week 12: the ethics boundary

The lecture and deck still say that making no eye contact falls outside
ordinary behaviour, and that a partner noticing an experiment proves its
parameter is outside that range. These are existing teaching rules, not merely
awkward sentences, so the draft preserves them and flags them here.

Both rules deserve reconsideration before adoption. A person's ordinary eye
contact can be none; a noticeable variation can be harmless. The course could
ask instead whether the proposed action pressures, deceives, identifies or
exposes the other person, and what happens if they object. That would be an
ethics-policy revision, requiring agreement across the lab questions, lecture,
deck and assessment brief. It is not silently included in this copy edit.

Locations: `src/content/lectures/week-12.mdx`, the field-experiment discussion
and final route exercise; `src/decks/week-12.deck.mdx`, the corresponding
ethics and route slides.

### Week 2: an observation column that contains a counterfactual

The “Gets you a theory / Gets you an observation” table includes a question
about what someone *would* do. The draft tells students to check interview
answers against exchanges, but a separate methods review should decide
whether that table heading overstates what the interview obtains. This is not
resolved by substituting a more confident sentence.

### Week 12: repeated closing material

The deck repeats the four-part course overview and the fluency-plus-choice
close around the capstone exercise. All slide boundaries and activities remain
in the draft. A later delivery pass could decide whether the repeated close
works as a return after the exercise or feels like ending twice. It should
remove or move slides deliberately, not pad the second close with synonyms.

### Research working notes after adoption

The research notes and authoring harness were context for this review, not
student prose. If these proposals are adopted, update those notes where they
still overstate the recovery-hours, role-switching, institutional-intervention
or readability interpretations. Otherwise a future writing pass may restore
the claims this draft has qualified.
