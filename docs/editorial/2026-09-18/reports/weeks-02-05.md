## Main choices

- **Week 2:** retain the maintenance failure metaphor and concrete field
  notes. Remove the paragraph praising the course's own honesty, repeated
  explanations of what the example demonstrates, and predictions about first
  submissions. A journal entry may describe a successful exchange. Hinds's
  recent-learner application is now a teaching suggestion, with the
  technical-task boundary retained. The Malle passage no longer calls
  negative events the only condition under which the effect appeared.
- **Week 3:** keep the awkward coffee invitation, decoder, practical scripts
  and deniability. Replace guaranteed readings of students and senders with
  things to check. Remove the unsupported promise that a sender will always
  retry. The self-check, diagnostic note and recap now include both uncertainty
  and leaving a mutually understood request off the record, matching the
  higher-order-deniability section and the reading annotation. The spoken hint
  is distinguished from the unsaid request: a hint can be quoted, even when
  its intended request remains disputable.
- **Week 4:** let the stopwatch exercise discover the room's results. Keep the
  timings and research caveats, while removing invented thousands of
  conversations and claims about an entire country's configuration. Hold
  tokens are proposals to try, with no promise that a sentence resets an
  entire relationship. The exchange photograph's caption describes visible
  arrangements rather than the operators' thoughts or attention. The shorter
  analogy keeps the supervisor joke. The deck now describes the lab's actual
  disclosed rounds instead of a covert hold-token test.
- **Week 5:** retain the apology template's candid correction, including the
  missing “I'm sorry”. Remove repeated claims that the course has thereby
  demonstrated its integrity. Practice does not promise automatic fluency,
  and students need not invent an omitted apology part. The cost table is
  marked as a proposal to test. Week 4's “most frequent” repair gap is carried
  consistently into week 5 rather than recast as a majority of cases.

Across the decks, teacher notes no longer script student agreement, mistakes,
surprise or the result of an activity. They direct attention to an example or
ask a question. Slide boundaries, headings and activity timings are preserved.

- Week 3's specification author filing the bug report, “politeness in a lab
  coat”, the absent answer key, the coffee clarification script, and the
  distinction between wanting a decoder and accepting all the blame.
- Week 4's configuration metaphor, “Some of the silence aimed at you is
  patience”, the reply/hold-token scripts and the gap-as-courtesy explanation.
- Week 5's incomplete uptime logs, steady-state traffic, optional “sorry”,
  faintly absurd missing apology, deletion by “but”, and permission to ask
  for time before accepting an apology.

These are specific jokes, useful contrasts or usable instructions. The draft
does not treat their shape as evidence of AI authorship.

## Questions outside this editorial pass

- Week 2's “Gets you a theory / Gets you an observation” table includes a
  counterfactual question in its observation column. The new instruction says
  to test either answer against exchanges, but the method's claimed connection
  to Nisbett and Wilson would benefit from a separate source review.
- Week 5 uses Kendrick's time to *initiate* repair to support “repairs faster”.
  That is not, by itself, a measurement of total repair time. The exact turn
  counts in the deck's cost table are also not established in the research
  record; they are now explicitly proposed for testing. No new empirical
  findings have been invented to support them.
- The Fehr paragraph's “what you do and say ... matters more than who the other
  person happens to be” remains broader than its quoted variance comparison.
  A source review should distinguish population-level explanation from a
  prediction about a particular apology. Speaker notes now explicitly avoid
  promising forgiveness.
