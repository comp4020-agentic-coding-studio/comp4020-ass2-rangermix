## OI-1: Romance

**Status:** open. **Out of scope because:** every earlier week at once, with the highest cost of error.

Five styles of communicating romantic interest --- traditional, physical, sincere, playful, polite --- from over five thousand adults. Physical, sincere and playful correlated with more dating success.

<span class="source">Hall et al. (2010), Communication Quarterly 58(4)</span>

```notes
- The inventory measures how people say they signal interest, not what receivers understand. That gap matters.
- The course's inference, not the paper's: if there are five styles, "I can't tell when someone's flirting" is sometimes a style mismatch. Week 3's decoder handles sincere well and playful badly.
```

---

## OI-1, asked of the people concerned

31 autistic and 26 non-autistic people, on what makes intimacy possible and what blocks it.

**Enablers, named by both:** communication; sharing and similarity; respect and safety for self and other; working on the relationship.

<span class="source">Sala, Hooley & Stokes (2020), JADD 50(11)</span>

```notes
- Distinguish the intimacy study from the self-reported flirting inventory.
- Both groups also named conflict as a barrier -- within a person and between people.
```

## OI-2: Persuasion

**Status:** open. **Out of scope because:** the protocol used deliberately to move someone, where the ethics are the hard part.

Research on compliance organised around three goals people already pursue: seeing things **accurately**, keeping their **relationships**, feeling good about **themselves**.

Much of the influence described is subtle, indirect, and outside the target's awareness.

<span class="source">Cialdini & Goldstein (2004), Annual Review of Psychology 55</span>

<MarginNote label="Security note">A protocol that works outside awareness is, by definition, an attack surface.</MarginNote>

```notes
- Everything in weeks 1 to 11 can be used to persuade. This course taught it so they could interoperate.
- That's why the Field Experiment's ethics reflection is graded, not an appendix.
```

## So the ethics reflection is graded

Route two varies a protocol parameter on people who never agreed to be in a study. The reflection carries 35%: account for what you do to them.

Thursday's lab reviews every parameter against **the four questions**:

1. **Is it ordinary?** Would it pass as something people do anyway?
2. **Who is it on?** And why is that group appropriate?
3. **What could it cost them?** Specifically --- and how would you notice?
4. **What stops it?** When do you stop varying and repair?

```notes
- Garfinkel’s method deliberately breached expectancies. This experiment keeps variations inside ordinary behaviour; students may be working with people they will see again.
- The course’s current rule is: "less eye contact than usual" is ordinary; "none at all" is a breach.
```

## In the room: check your parameter (7 min)

**Three minutes, alone.** Write the parameter you'd vary, as a number or a behaviour.

**Three minutes, in pairs.** Your partner runs the four questions against it, out loud, in order.

**One minute.** Whose parameter failed a question? Hands up. Use the failed question to revise it before Thursday.

```notes
- Watch for failures on question 1. "I'll stop making eye contact entirely" is outside the range of ordinary behaviour; "less than usual" is inside it.
- Route-one students run the same check on somebody else's, because reading a design is how you learn to write one.
```

## OI-3: Power

**Status:** open, and **contested**. **Out of scope because:** it changes every other parameter, and the research is still arguing.

Elevated power activates **approach**: more positive affect, more automatic processing, more disinhibited behaviour.

Reduced power activates **inhibition**: more attention to threat and to **other people's interests**, more controlled processing.

<span class="source">Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson (2003), Psychological Review 110(2)</span>

```notes
- The account has drawn published criticism. Treat it as a proposal, not a settled result, and say so.
```

---

## "Contested" is a gesture

Which claim can the existing studies test: that high and low power differ, or how each affects behaviour?

**Moskowitz (2004), Psychological Review 111(3), 808--811.**

```notes
- Keep the comment beside the proposal as you explain what it challenges.
```

## What the objection actually is

**Not** "the proposal is false."

The studies <span data-quoted>have permitted examining the opposite effects but not the specific effects of high and low power</span>.

They can show high and low power differing. Not that **each half** does what it separately claims.

And the comment sets out the designs that **would** settle it.

```notes
- Read as an engineer: it names the claim, says what the evidence cannot distinguish, and specifies the test that would close it.
- Published next to the thing it criticises, under a name, where anyone can find it.
```
