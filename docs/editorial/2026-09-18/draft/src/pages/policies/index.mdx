# Policies and support

A course that argues for documentation owes you its own. Everything below is
the specification: what's required, what isn't, and what to do when something
goes wrong.

## Entry

No prior study is needed. Entry is by **three references from people who know
you well**, such as a teacher, an employer, a family member or a friend. Each
one attests either that you would benefit from this course, or that you're
interested in how human relationships and communication work.

No diagnosis is required, and none is accepted as evidence.

Both kinds of reference count. You do not have to say whether the protocol
comes easily to you.

## Extensions

- **Up to seven days**, once per assessment, for any reason, with no
  documentation, if you ask **before** the deadline. Use week 8's specimen:
  ask for a date.
- **Longer than seven days**, or a second extension on the same task: email the
  convenor, who will ask what date you need and what's getting in the way. You
  don't need to explain more than the need.
- **The Field Journal** has no extensions. Your lowest of eleven submissions
  is dropped.

Work submitted late without an extension loses 5% of its available marks per
day, for up to five days, and isn't accepted after that.

## Adjustments

If something about the course costs you more than it should, like a loud lab,
a spoken-only instruction, or a deadline that collides with something outside
your control, tell your tutor or the convenor. **You can tell us the need
without the label.** Week 10 calls that partial disclosure, and this course
accepts it without asking for anything further.

Slop University's Accessibility Service can arrange formal adjustments across
all your courses, such as exam arrangements. That service may ask for
documentation for a formal plan; that's its process, not this course's.

Every lab has a version you can do from the edge of the room, and weeks 7 and
10 have explicit opt-outs. Tell your tutor at the start of the lab, and you
don't need to say why.

## Asking for help

The escalation path, in order:

1. **Your tutor**, for anything about a lab, the journal, or a single week's
   content. Replies within two working days.
2. **The convenor**, for assessment, extensions, adjustments, or anything your
   tutor couldn't resolve. If your tutor hasn't replied in two working days,
   email the convenor and say so. Using the escalation path is not rude.
3. **Slop University Student Services**, for anything beyond this course.

Use the course code and the request in the email subject line: `[NT101] topic: one-line request`. Week 8 has the whole specification.

## If you need support now

The course deals with burnout (week 7) and disclosure (week 10). If either one
lands harder than a lecture should, talk to someone.

If you're thinking about suicide, or you're not safe, tell someone today. In
Australia you can call **Lifeline on 13 11 14**, or text **0477 13 11 14**, at
any time. In an emergency, call **000**.

Slop University Counselling offers free, confidential appointments to every
enrolled student, and your tutor or the convenor will help you get in touch
with it.

## Using AI

Talk to an AI about your observations as much as you like: to think a rule
through, to find the maxim a sentence bends, to argue with your draft
specification. **The writing you submit must be yours.**

The Field Journal has no word limit or minimum.

## Observing other people

Field methods involve other people, who didn't sign up for this course.

- **Write down words, not people.** No names, no descriptions that identify
  anyone, in any submission.
- **No recordings.** No audio, no video, no screenshots of anyone else's
  messages.
- **Public or yours.** Observe exchanges in public spaces, or exchanges you took
  part in.
- **The Field Experiment** varies one parameter inside the range of ordinary
  behaviour, and its ethics reflection is graded. Week 12's lab reviews every
  proposal before it runs.

## Academic integrity

Your observations, your rules and your writing are yours. Quote sources, cite
them, and don't present a reading's finding as your own observation, or your
own observation as a finding. Each reading lists what it was checked against.

## Grading

No penalty for grammar, spelling or presentation, in any assessment, beyond
whether the work can be read. The four assessments and their weights are on
the [assessment page](/assessments/).
