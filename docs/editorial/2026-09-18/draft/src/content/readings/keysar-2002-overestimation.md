Participants said ambiguous sentences while trying to convey one specific intention, then estimated whether their addressee had understood. They consistently **overestimated**: they expected to be understood more often than they actually were. **Overhearers who knew the speaker's intention did not systematically overestimate**. Being the one who spoke is what produces the error.

The authors' account: speakers monitoring their own utterances <span data-quoted>do not act as unbiased observers</span>. They underestimate how ambiguous their own sentence was, and overestimate how much their own disambiguating cues made the intention transparent. They close by calling this a systematic source of miscommunication in ordinary conversation.

Week 3 uses this when asking senders to check how a hint landed. Confidence in the intended meaning does not tell the sender what the other person understood.
