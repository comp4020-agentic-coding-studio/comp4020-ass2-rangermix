Autistic respondents and those aware of the neurodiversity movement tended to see autism as an identity rather than a condition to be removed. Yet on several questions, people with different relations to autism answered alike. The course reads it as a guard against its own slogans: the framing this course takes is contested, and the people it concerns hold more than one view at once.
