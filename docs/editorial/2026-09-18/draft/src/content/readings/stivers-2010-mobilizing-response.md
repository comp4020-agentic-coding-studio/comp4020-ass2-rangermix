How does one person get another to respond, and when is the other person accountable for not responding? Stivers and Rossano test two inherited answers — that interrogativity does it, and that the social action being performed does it — and conclude that each has merit and neither settles the puzzle. Their account is a gradient: different actions mobilise response to different degrees, and speakers design turns with response-mobilising features that hold a recipient more or less accountable for replying. Week 1 uses this gradient to distinguish how strongly different openings invite a reply. Obligation turns out to be a dial, not a switch, which is also why the same four words are a passing token in a corridor and a real enquiry in a clinic.
