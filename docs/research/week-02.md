# Week 2 review --- the undocumented majority, and field notes without pathology

**Question.** Why does nobody who runs the protocol write it down? And when a
conversation fails between two people who run it differently, whose failure is
it?

## What the sources say

Garfinkel explains the missing documentation. The rules a person runs are
background expectancies, so they are invisible to the person running them
until someone breaks one. Nobody specifies what they cannot see.

Milton relocates the failure. When an autistic and a non-autistic person
misunderstand each other, the misunderstanding is mutual. Treating it as one
party's missing capacity is a choice of frame, not a finding.

Sasson et al. put a number on the majority's side of that. Observers judged
autistic people less favourably from seconds of video. When they judged the
*content* of what was said, stripped of audio-visual style, the gap went away.

Kapp et al. show the frame is contested rather than settled. Their
respondents often held the difference view and parts of the medical view at
once.

## Where they disagree

- Milton's is a theoretical piece; its evidence came later (see week 11).
- Sasson et al.'s abstract uses the clinical vocabulary the course avoids,
  while its finding points the other way: the negative judgement tracks style,
  not substance. The course cites the finding and does not adopt the
  vocabulary.
- Kapp et al. complicate any neat "difference, not deficit" slogan. The course
  keeps that complication rather than flattening it.

## What survives into teaching

- The protocol is undocumented because it is background, not because it is
  obvious (Garfinkel). This is the mechanism behind the course's governing
  sentence.
- A cross-implementation failure is a mismatch with two sides (Milton).
- Style is judged before content (Sasson et al.). That motivates the field
  note's first rule: record what was said and done before any judgement of
  how it came across.
- Field notes describe; they don't diagnose. The before/after rewrite
  exercise comes from here.
