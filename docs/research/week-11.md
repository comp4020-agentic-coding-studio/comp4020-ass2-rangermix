# Week 11 review --- interoperation, not conversion

**Question.** When two implementations meet, where does the breakdown live,
and what does the evidence say about fixing it from one side versus both?

## Sources and how each was checked

| Reading | Checked against | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crompton, Ropar, Evans-Williams, Flynn & Fletcher-Watson (2020), Autistic peer-to-peer information transfer is highly effective, *Autism* 24(7) | abstract (Crossref), 2026-09-15 | diffusion chains of eight (N = 72): detail declined significantly more steeply in mixed chains; autistic chains did not differ significantly from non-autistic chains; rapport was lower in mixed chains |
| Crompton, Hallett, Ropar, Flynn & Fletcher-Watson (2020), "I never realised everybody felt as happy as I do when I am around autistic people", *Autism* 24(6) | abstract (Crossref), 2026-09-15 | 12 autistic adults: themes of cross-neurotype understanding, minority status and belonging; to spend time with neurotypical friends and family, participants often had to conform to what those people were used to |
| Sheppard et al. (2016), How easy is it to read the minds of people with autism spectrum disorder?, *JADD* 46(4) | abstract (Semantic Scholar), 2026-09-15 | neurotypical observers were less accurate at identifying what autistic targets were reacting to; autistic targets were rated equally expressive for most events, so they reacted differently, not less expressively |
| DeBrabander et al. (2019), Do first impressions of autistic adults differ between autistic and nonautistic observers?, *Autism in Adulthood* 1(4) | abstract (OpenAlex), 2026-09-15 | autistic raters shared the less favourable trait judgements of autistic adults, but those judgements did not reduce their interest in interacting, as they did for non-autistic raters |
| Rifai, Fletcher-Watson, Jiménez-Sánchez & Crompton (2022), Investigating markers of rapport in autistic and nonautistic interactions, *Autism in Adulthood* 4(1) | abstract (Crossref), 2026-09-15 | mutual gaze and backchannelling were lower in mixed pairs than non-autistic pairs, matching lower rapport; but less backchannelling in autistic pairs went with *higher* rapport; the visible markers of rapport differ by neurotype |
| Mitchell, Sheppard & Cassidy (2021), Autism and the double empathy problem: implications for development and mental health, *British Journal of Developmental Psychology* 39(1) | abstract (Crossref), 2026-09-15 | a transactional account: the majority's misperceptions shape autistic people's behaviour and isolation, with costs to mental health and to wider society; opposes accounts of autism as diminished social motivation |

## What the sources say

The evidence now backs Milton's 2012 theory (week 2). Information survives
within either neurotype and degrades between them (Crompton et al., peer
transfer). Autistic people's reactions are misread by others rather than
missing (Sheppard et al.). Even the signals of rapport are
implementation-specific: what counts as "engaged" in one pairing is noise in
another (Rifai et al.). The cost of the mismatch lands mostly on the minority,
who conform to be included (Crompton et al., relationships), with long-term
consequences (Mitchell et al.).

## Where they disagree

- DeBrabander et al. complicate a clean "same-neurotype understanding" story:
  autistic raters made similar trait judgements to non-autistic raters, and
  differed in what they did with them.
- Sample sizes are small in the qualitative work (12) and moderate in the
  experimental work (72).

## What survives into teaching

- *Interoperation*, the week's term: two implementations agreeing a shared
  protocol for the exchange at hand, as opposed to *conversion*, where one
  implementation replaces itself.
- The breakdown is in the mismatch, so the fix is bilateral. Every source
  above locates the loss between people, not inside one of them.
- Rapport markers are implementation-specific (Rifai et al.). Negotiating
  which markers count is part of interoperating.
- The class itself is the worked example: eleven weeks of shared vocabulary
  is a negotiated protocol.

## Stated as the course's own observation, not research

- The bilateral protocol document format and the lab's negotiation procedure.
- The claim that a mixed room with shared terms interoperates better. It is
  the course's thesis, demonstrated in the room, not a tested finding.
