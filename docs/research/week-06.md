# Week 6 review --- masking: cost accounting

**Question.** What is masking, who does it, what does it buy, and what does it
cost?

## Sources and how each was checked

## What the sources say

Masking works: people do it to fit in and connect (Hull et al.). It has a
price: exhaustion and threats to self-perception (Hull et al.), and poorer
mental health for heavy or switching use (Cage & Troxell-Whitman). Pearson &
Rose argue that "choosing" to mask is constrained by stigma, which makes the
choice less free than it looks. And it isn't only autistic people:
non-autistic students camouflage too, with distress attached (O'Loghlen &
Lang), and whole occupations are paid to manage their feelings on display
(Hochschild).

## Where they disagree

- Hull et al. and Cage & Troxell-Whitman measure costs within autistic
  samples. O'Loghlen & Lang frame non-autistic camouflaging partly as low
  social competence, a framing Pearson & Rose would likely resist. The course
  presents O'Loghlen & Lang's finding (the practice and its distress are
  shared), not their competence framing.
- The design says "taught as a budget". The literature doesn't use budget
  language; that is the course's frame, and it has to be honest that the costs
  are associations, not measured prices.
- Pearson & Rose's "illusion of choice" pushes against a naive "just decide
  when to mask" lesson. The course keeps it: the budget is real, and so is the
  pressure on it.

## What survives into teaching

- Masking is a subroutine you can run, with a cost you can estimate. The week
  never presents it as an aspiration or as a failing.
- The ledger: what each masked subroutine buys and what it costs, per
  context. Switching contexts has its own cost (Cage & Troxell-Whitman).
- The room is mixed: everyone runs some managed performance, and the costs
  differ by implementation (O'Loghlen & Lang; Hochschild).
- Choice is constrained by stigma (Pearson & Rose). The ledger records the
  pressure alongside the cost.

## Stated as the course's own observation, not research

- The ledger format and its units: an hour of recovery per subroutine is a
  planning unit, not a measured quantity.
- The weekly budget framing that week 7 builds on.
