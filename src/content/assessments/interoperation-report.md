> Agree a protocol with someone who runs a different one, and write down what
> it cost you both.

In week 9's lab you meet a partner the tutor chose, deliberately, to mix the
room. Over the labs of weeks 9, 10 and 11, and the working weeks in between,
the two of you negotiate a shared protocol for how you work together: reply
times, how either of you says "I need a pause", what depth your conversations
stay at, and how either of you flags a mismatch.

Then each of you, separately, reports what happened.

## What a strong response does

It takes week 11 seriously: **the fix is bilateral**. A report in which one
partner conceded everything describes conversion, not interoperation, and
should say so plainly if that's what happened. That can still be a strong
report, if it's honest about it.

It reports **disagreement as data**. If you and your partner remember the
concessions differently, the difference is the most interesting part of the
report. Don't resolve it; describe it.

It uses the course's **terms exactly**: payload, timeout, rate limit, mismatch.
A report that invents its own vocabulary for things the course already named
is doing the harder version of the task.

## What you submit

- **The final bilateral protocol**, in the week 11 format: clause, who
  concedes, how either of you checks it.
- **A report of 1000--1500 words**, written by you alone: what you conceded,
  what your partner conceded, what held, what didn't, and why.

Each partner submits their own report. You may share the protocol document; you
may not share the report.

If the partnership stops working, tell Jonah by the end of week 10's lab. A
renegotiated partnership is a legitimate thing to report.
