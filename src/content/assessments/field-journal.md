> Once a week, catch the protocol doing something, and write down the rule.

The Field Journal is the course's main instrument. Every lecture gives you a
part of the protocol to look for, every lab gives you practice looking, and
every Sunday you turn one real exchange into a rule you can test.

It starts in week 2 and runs to week 12: eleven submissions, each worth 3%.
**Your best ten count**, so one week is yours to drop. That's deliberate. A
course that teaches masking as a budget, and then punishes one overspent week,
isn't taking its own week 6 seriously.

## What an entry looks like

Each entry has four parts, the shape of a good engineering retrospective:

1. **What happened.** One concrete exchange: who (by role, not name), where,
   and what was said and done.
2. **The rule you inferred.** Stated so someone who wasn't there could run it.
3. **The test.** A second time you checked the rule, including if it failed.
4. **The rule now.** Revised or held, and why.

A short example, from week 4's material:

> **What happened.** Tuesday, group meeting of five. I asked "can everyone do
> Thursday 2pm?" Two people said "yes" straight away. One said "…yeah, should
> be fine" after a pause. That person didn't come on Thursday.
>
> **The rule.** In this group, a yes after a noticeable pause means "probably
> not, but I don't want to say so in front of everyone."
>
> **The test.** Friday, I asked the same person one-to-one, by message: "Is
> Thursday actually good for you? Totally fine if not." Reply: "Honestly Monday
> is better."
>
> **The rule now.** Held, with an addition: the pause means *not in front of
> the group*. Ask again, privately, with an explicit exit.

## What you submit

One file per week to the course page, containing at least one entry. Write as
much as you need: **fifty words or five thousand are both fine**. There is no
word limit and no minimum.

**Grammar, spelling and presentation are not marked.** Talk your observations
through with an AI as much as you like. The writing must be yours, and that is
exactly why the format is unconstrained: a fixed word count is what makes
generated text convenient to submit.

## When it's due

At 23:59 Canberra time on each Sunday of teaching weeks 2 to 12. Nothing is due
over the two-week break. The full list of dates is above.
