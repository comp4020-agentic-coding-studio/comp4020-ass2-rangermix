A mini-review of the literature on self-advocacy and self-determination in autistic adolescents and young adults, framed by the biosocial model of resilience and the neurodiversity movement. Its findings “point to encouraging associations” of self-advocacy and self-determination with educational and employment outcomes, socialisation, relationship development, identity development, self-concept and quality of life. The hedge in that phrasing is the authors' own and the week keeps it: these are associations reported in a short review, not a demonstrated effect of teaching anybody to write an email.

Week 8 uses this to justify spending an hour on institutional scripts at all. Asking well is associated with outcomes that matter, so the skill is worth having. Read beside Davies & Bagnall, though, it sets up the week's real position: self-advocacy helps the person doing it, *and* a system that works only for students who can do it has pushed its own cost onto them.
