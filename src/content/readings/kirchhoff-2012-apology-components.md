Two experimental vignette studies in Germany. Study 1 found that including more verbal elements in an apology increased forgiveness, especially after a more severe offence, and that the relationship between an apology's completeness and forgiveness was *mediated by anger reduction* — a mechanism week 5 previously had none of. Study 2 found that for a more severe offence four elements were particularly relevant: conveying emotions, admitting fault, a statement of apology such as “I apologize,” and an attempt at explanation.

That last one is why this reading changed the course rather than decorating it. Week 5's template had no explicit statement of apology at all, and told students to keep explanation out. The template now carries both, with the explanation placed after the ownership and kept shorter than it — which is the course's actual objection, stated precisely instead of as a ban. Two vignette studies in one country is not the last word, and the week says so.
