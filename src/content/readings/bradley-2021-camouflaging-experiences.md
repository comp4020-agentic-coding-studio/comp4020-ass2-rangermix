An online survey designed in partnership with autistic adults, analysed for 277 respondents, asking where they camouflage and how much time they spend doing it. Three themes came out. The “dangers of camouflaging” theme tied the *amount of time spent* to exhaustion, isolation, poor mental and physical health, loss of identity and self-acceptance, others' unreal expectations, and delayed diagnosis. The “positive aspects” theme named greater access to social spaces and protection from harm — camouflaging seen as necessary to survive in a world designed for the neurotypical majority. The third theme was being diagnosed and accepted as a reason not to camouflage as much as before.
