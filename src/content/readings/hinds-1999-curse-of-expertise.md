Experts, intermediate users and novices predicted how long a complex task would take a novice; in the second study, expertise was manipulated rather than measured. In both studies, **more expertise went with worse prediction**, in the direction of underestimating completion time. The participants were **resistant to debiasing techniques** aimed at that underestimation. The checked abstract does not identify those techniques. It suggests that people with an **intermediate** level of expertise may be more accurate predictors.

For week 2, the distinction is between doing a task and estimating what it takes another person to do it. The studies concern technical-task timings. They do not measure the cost of learning a conversational convention.

For week 2, try asking someone who still remembers learning the convention. This is a teaching suggestion: Hinds timed a technical task, not a conversation. A fluent speaker's “it's obvious” need not be a reliable estimate of what learning it will cost you.
