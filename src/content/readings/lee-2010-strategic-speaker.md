The strategic speaker hints because they are <span data-quoted>uncertain of whether the hearer is cooperative or antagonistic</span>. The abstract's example is bribing a policeman who may be corrupt or honest. In the model, a veiled bribe can be taken up by the corrupt one or passed over by the honest one. Ordinary interactions have the same payoff structure with emotional penalties instead of legal ones, whenever the request is implicitly forbidden by the kind of relationship the two people are in.

**Even when the hearer's willingness is known**, indirectness still buys something: higher-order deniability, which the authors describe as preempting certainty, gossip, and common knowledge of the request. A hint that both parties fully understand is still not a thing either of them said.

For week 3, ask what the wording lets either person decline or leave off the record. Uncertainty about the answer is one possibility; the known-willingness case above is another reason to stay indirect.
