Sixty-seven autistic and 58 non-autistic participants were put into pairs — both autistic, both non-autistic, or mixed — and had a five-minute unstructured conversation with someone they had never met. Then each rated the conversation and their partner.

Autistic adults were rated <span data-quoted>more awkward, less attractive, and less socially warm</span> by autistic and non-autistic partners alike. The judgement travelled across both groups.

**Only the non-autistic adults' interest in a future interaction followed the judgement.** Autistic participants trended towards preferring other autistic partners, and reported **disclosing more about themselves** to autistic than to non-autistic partners.

Week 11 distinguishes an unfavourable rating from a decision not to meet again. It also uses the disclosure result to ask what changes with the partner. Week 9 raises a related question through Kluger and Malloy's argument that much of the variance in question-asking belongs to the particular pair rather than the person.
