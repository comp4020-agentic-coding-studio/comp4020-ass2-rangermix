Nobody discloses anything real in this lab. Every disclosure belongs to a
character on a card, so you can practise without giving the room information
about yourself.

## Before the lab

Bring your week 9 draft protocol with your partner.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:05 | Character cards handed out: a need, a context, two audiences (*needs written instructions; starting a part-time job; a supervisor and a coworker*). |
| 0:05--0:20 | **Draft.** For your character, write a need-without-label sentence for each audience. Different audiences often need different sentences. |
| 0:20--0:32 | **Say it and receive it.** In pairs: say one of your character's sentences. Your partner receives it using the lecture's three steps: thank, ask about the need, don't re-read. Swap. Tell each other which step, if any, went missing. |
| 0:32--0:37 | As a room: which receiving step was hardest to hold back from breaking? |
| 0:37--0:55 | **Interoperation Report, round 2.** With your partner: take last week's three draft clauses and mark each one **held**, **revised** or **dropped**, with a reason. Add up to two new clauses from this week's working together. |
| 0:55--1:00 | Each partner writes, separately, one clause they conceded on. |

## If this week is close to home

It might be. The character cards are there so you can practise at one remove.
You can also sit this lab's first half out entirely, by telling Jonah at the
start, and join for the Interoperation Report round. Nothing about this lab's
spec requires you to share anything about yourself.

## What leaves the room

Two draft sentences for a character, receiving practice, and a revised draft
protocol. If you choose to say a need-without-label sentence of your own this
week, you can use it for the Field Journal: what you said, to whom, what came
back.
