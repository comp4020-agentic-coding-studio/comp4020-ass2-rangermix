Tuesday gave you three repair initiators and an apology template. Today you
practise both with a partner who checks what they heard.

## Before the lab

Nothing to prepare. The template is on the lecture page; bring it on a screen.
You can run the same rounds by passing written lines at the edge of the room.
An acceptance is one written or spoken turn; the role counts stay the same.

## Run sheet

Times below run from the 14:05 start; finish at 14:55.

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:06 | Pairs. Read the card instructions below and demonstrate one repair. |
| 0:06--0:22 | **Repair round.** Run the six cards twice: first A sends all six and B repairs, then swap. The sender supplies the missing information after the most specific available initiator; the listener accepts in one turn. Mark initiator type, not whether it was the tutor's favourite. |
| 0:22--0:27 | Compare one specific and one less specific repair. What information was available when the listener chose? If all were specific, explain what made that possible. |
| 0:27--0:43 | **Apology round.** Two each, using the two scenarios below. The partner ticks the template parts they heard, then responds in one turn. "Thanks, I need time" is allowed. Use the second attempt to include any missing part. |
| 0:43--0:50 | Pool the missing-part tally. Each person writes one part to watch for in a real exchange, and a possible second instance for Sunday's journal. |

## Repair cards

The sender reads the line, keeping the intended detail hidden until asked.
Both partners may know the bank; this is practice choosing an initiator.

| Spoken line | Detail for the sender to supply |
| --- | --- |
| "Meet you Thursday." | Thursday next week |
| "Send it to the other address." | the course email, not the group chat |
| "Put it beside that one." | beside the blue notebook |
| "We start at two." | two in the afternoon |
| "Bring the earlier version." | the draft dated Monday |
| "Ask the tutor after the thing." | after the lecture |

## Apology scenarios

Both partners practise both fictional cases:

- You promised to bring a printed worksheet and forgot. Your partner had to
  share a screen. You can print a copy during the next break.
- You sent a group the wrong room number. One person walked to another floor.
  You have now checked the room and corrected the message.

Use the lecture's seven-row template. The receiver checks for the parts, not
for a performance of remorse. A brief explanation is allowed after ownership;
it does not erase what the other person had to do.

## A note on the apology round

The scenarios are trivial on purpose. Nobody practises an apology for
something they're actually sorry about in a lab. The point is the structure,
not the feeling, so that on the day you need it, the parts are already there.

If building an apology from parts feels mechanical: it is, for now. Week 1's
handshake felt mechanical too, written down. The parts are what the
unmechanical version is made of.

## What leaves the room

A tally of your repair initiators, and a note of any apology parts you left
out. Choose one part to test in Sunday's journal.
