Tuesday gave you three repair initiators and an apology template. Today you
use both until they come out without planning.

## Before the lab

Nothing to prepare. The template is on the lecture page; bring it on a screen.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:08 | Pairs. Each pair gets a deck of **planted** cards: a message with one ambiguity inside ("Can you send it to her before the thing?"). |
| 0:08--0:28 | **Repair round.** A reads a card aloud. B repairs it with the most specific initiator they can. A scores it **open**, **restricted request** or **restricted offer**, and gives the fix. B accepts in one turn. Swap every card. Aim for eight each. |
| 0:28--0:33 | Tally on the board: how many restricted offers did the room manage? |
| 0:33--0:50 | **Apology round.** Scenario cards, all low stakes: *you replied to the group chat three days late*; *you took the last good seat without asking*. Give the apology from the template. Your partner ticks each part they heard, and circles any "but". Then they accept in one turn. Two each. |
| 0:50--1:00 | Which part got left out most across the room? Discuss why that one. |

## A note on the apology round

The scenarios are trivial on purpose. Nobody practises an apology for
something they're actually sorry about in a lab. The point is the structure,
not the feeling, so that on the day you need it, the parts are already there.

If building an apology from parts feels mechanical: it is, for now. Week 1's
handshake felt mechanical too, written down. The parts are what the
unmechanical version is made of.

## What leaves the room

A tally of your repair initiators, and a note of the apology part you left
out. That part is your journal rule for Sunday.
