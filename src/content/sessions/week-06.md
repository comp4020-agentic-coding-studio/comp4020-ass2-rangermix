Tuesday introduced the ledger. Today you fill one in, and find out how much
the same subroutine costs the people sitting next to you.

## Before the lab

Think back over yesterday. No writing needed in advance.

## Your ledger is yours

You choose what goes on the table. Any row you'd rather keep private can go in
as "private", with just a cost and a pressure. Groups compare **ratings of
common subroutines**, never each other's ledgers. Nobody reads your ledger
except you, and your tutor if you ask them to.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:15 | **Yesterday's ledger**, alone. Five rows minimum, in the lecture's format, including any switching charge. |
| 0:15--0:20 | Groups of four, mixed by the tutor. Each group gets a card of common subroutines: *eye contact while listening*; *small talk before a class starts*; *answering "how are you?" with a token*; *staying in a loud room*; *laughing at a joke you didn't find funny*; *saying you're fine when you're not*. |
| 0:20--0:38 | **Rate the card.** Each person privately rates each subroutine's cost from 0 (free) to 5 (the rest of my day). Then reveal together, and write the spread for each subroutine. |
| 0:38--0:50 | Groups post their widest spread to the board. The room looks for the subroutines everyone finds cheap, and the ones where a zero and a five sat at the same table. |
| 0:50--1:00 | **Plan one decline.** Pick a row from your ledger to decline once before next Tuesday. Write its expected cost beside it. |

## What to look for on the board

Look for subroutines that cost almost everyone almost nothing, and for any that
split a table between a zero and a five. The splits, if the room has them, are week 6's argument happening in
front of you: different implementations, same output, different price. They're
also why "just make eye contact" is advice that costs its giver nothing.

## What leaves the room

Your ledger and your planned decline. Try the decline this week. The
journal entry is what happened, the rule you inferred about its cost, and
whether the cost was the one you wrote down.
