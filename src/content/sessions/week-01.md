Tuesday's lecture wrote down the opening handshake. Today you check the
specification against the building.

## Before the lab

Bring a phone or a notebook. Nothing to read in advance; the lecture is
enough.

If a corridor at ten past the hour is the wrong kind of day for you, tell
Tamsin at the start. The edge-of-the-room version logs only the openings you
overhear from a seat by the door, and counts the same.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:10 | The log format, on the board. For each opening: who started it (A or B), the exact words, the reply type from the lecture's table, and whether it **closed** or **branched**. |
| 0:10--0:35 | Capture, in pairs. Walk the building's corridors, café queue and stairwells. Log openings you take part in, and openings you overhear in public spaces. |
| 0:35--0:50 | Pool the logs on the board. Tally the reply types. Circle every branch. |
| 0:50--1:00 | Look at the branches. For each one, what happened just before it? Did the asker stop moving, ask twice, or move somewhere quieter? Or something the lecture didn't list? |

## Rules for logging people

Write down words, not people. No names, no descriptions of anyone's
appearance, no audio recording. "Two students by the lift" is the most detail
a log needs. The course studies a protocol, not the people running it; they
didn't agree to be in this lab.

## What leaves the room

Your log, and the pooled tally, which goes up on the course page on Friday.
If the branch signals from the lecture didn't hold up, that is a result: the
signals are the course's working rules, and a working rule is there to be
tested.

Keep the log. Next week it becomes your first field note.
