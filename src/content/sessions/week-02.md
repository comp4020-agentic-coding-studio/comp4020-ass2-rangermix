The lecture gave you the four-part field note and the asleep test. Today you
use both on real observations, and have someone check your work.

## Before the lab

Bring week 1's corridor log, and something to write on that you're willing to
hand to a partner.

## Run sheet

| Time | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00--0:10 | The four-part format and the asleep test, with two sentences from last week's pooled log rewritten on the board. |
| 0:10--0:30 | Observe. Pick one public space: the café queue, the library entrance, a bus stop, the room you're sitting in. Write down exchanges as they happen: who by role, what was said, what happened next. No interpreting yet. |
| 0:30--0:40 | Swap notes with a partner. Underline every sentence that would still be true of the person if they were asleep. |
| 0:40--0:52 | Swap back and rewrite each underlined sentence as a description of an exchange. If you can't, the sentence wasn't an observation; cross it out. |
| 0:52--1:00 | From your rewritten notes, choose one rule to test before Sunday, and write it in a form someone else could run. |

## Why a partner reads your notes

The asleep test is easy to apply to someone else's sentence and hard to apply
to your own. An impression feels like an observation from the inside. That is
week 2's lecture happening at the scale of one notebook.

Partners are assigned by the tutor to mix the room. You'll swap with someone
whose notes read differently from yours. That difference is the point, and
it's the start of the shared vocabulary this class builds towards in week 11.

## What leaves the room

A page of notes that passes the asleep test, and one written-down rule. Test
the rule this week, and Sunday's journal entry writes itself: what happened,
the rule, the test, the rule now.
