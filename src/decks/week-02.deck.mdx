## Why the maintainers can't document it

Ordinary interaction runs on **background expectancies**: unstated assumptions that only show when someone breaches them.

When one is breached, people work to make things normal again, and that work reveals it.

<span class="source">Garfinkel (1964), Social Problems 11(3)</span>

<MarginNote label="A maintenance note">A system its maintainers can only debug by watching it crash.</MarginNote>

---

{/* _class: impact */}

"Obvious" is what background feels like from the inside.

```notes
- This is the mechanism behind the governing sentence: undocumented because background, not because obvious.
- The course's own observation: anyone whose implementation differs runs a small breach every day, unplanned.
```

## So ask them, and see what you get

Garfinkel says the rule is invisible.

He doesn't say what happens when you ask anyway --- which is that you **get an answer**.

```notes
- Set this up as an experiment they can run at dinner. Ask a fluent user why they did something social. You will not get silence.
```

## Where the answer comes from

Evidence for **little or no direct introspective access** to higher-order cognitive processes.

A person answering "why did you" isn't introspecting. They produce **a priori, implicit causal theories** --- judgements about what is a *plausible* cause.

<span class="source">Nisbett & Wilson (1977), Psychological Review 84(3)</span>

<MarginNote label="Bug report">"Cannot reproduce. Works on my machine."</MarginNote>

```notes
- Say the three unawarenesses: of the stimulus, of the response, of the link between them.
- Stress what this is not. Not evasion, not a bad memory. A more co-operative person hands you the same thing.
```

---

The answer is fluent, confident, and built on the spot.

```notes
- Which is why interviewing your way to the protocol does not work, and why this course observes instead.
```

## The clause everyone skips

Reports **will** sometimes be accurate --- when the influential thing is **salient** and a **plausible** cause.

| Gets you a theory | Gets you an observation |
| --- | --- |
| "Why do people say 'how are you'?" | "You just said 'good, you?' --- what if you'd said 'terrible'?" |
| "How do you know when to stop talking?" | "You stopped just then. What did she do first?" |

```notes
- This is the interview technique for the whole semester, and week 11's lab is built on it.
- The left column invites them to theorise about people in general. They will, at length, and it will not be the rule.
```

## In the room: ask a worse question, then a better one (9 min)

**Three minutes.** Ask your partner: *"How do you know when it's your turn to speak?"* Write the answer word for word.

**Three minutes.** Now ask about something that happened here in the last ten minutes.

**Three minutes, the room.** One pair of answers read out. What differs is the *kind* of answer.

```notes
- Nobody is being tested and nobody's first answer is wrong. The first question is a badly specified question; that is the finding.
- If a pair gets a good answer to the first question, that is interesting too -- ask what made it salient.
```

## And they can't tell how hard it is

Experts, intermediate users and novices predicted how long a complex task would take **a novice**.

**More expertise, worse prediction.** Underestimating, both studies, and **resistant to debiasing**.

<span class="source">Hinds (1999), Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied 5(2)</span>

```notes
- Being told about the bias did not repair the estimate. That is what makes it a finding rather than a saying.
- Scope: a technical task with a clock on it. Nobody timed a tutorial. Take the shape, not the setting.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

"It's obvious" is not evidence that a thing is easy. It is evidence about the speaker.

```notes
- To the fluent half of the room: it is a report on your own fluency, which is the variable that spoils the estimate -- and you have now been warned, which Hinds says does not reliably fix it.
- To the other half: this is why the answer "you just know" is not a brush-off. It is the honest output of a broken instrument.
```

## Who to ask instead

Hinds found **intermediate** expertise predicted more accurately than expert.

The classmate who worked out the tutorial last year **remembers the seams**. The one who never had to has nothing to remember.

```notes
- This is an argument about how the room is arranged, not a compliment to anyone in it.
- It is also why the labs are mixed on purpose. Week 11 is where that pays off.
```

## Whose failure is a mismatch?

The **double empathy problem**: a misunderstanding between two people of different implementations is mutual, a question of reciprocity.

<span class="source">Milton (2012), Disability & Society 27(6)</span>

---

## Style is judged before substance

Observers formed less favourable impressions of autistic people from seconds of behaviour, and were less willing to interact.

Judging only **what was said**, without audio-visual cues, the difference disappeared.

<span class="source">Sasson et al. (2017), Scientific Reports 7</span>

```notes
- The paper uses clinical vocabulary; its finding points at the observer. Cite the finding, not the vocabulary.
```

---

## The frame is contested

657 people surveyed: many held parts of the difference view and parts of the medical view at once.

The course's claim is narrow: a minority implementation of a convention is not a broken one.

<span class="source">Kapp et al. (2013), Developmental Psychology 49(1)</span>

## The argument this course is not going to make

The **actor--observer asymmetry**: we explain ourselves by the situation, other people by the person.

You were in a hurry; he is rude.

Famous. In every textbook. It would make the next rule sound inevitable.

```notes
- Set it up sincerely. Let them nod along, because they have all heard it.
```

## 173 studies

Average effect sizes: **d = -0.016 to d = 0.095**.

Described in the literature as <span data-quoted>robust, firmly established, and pervasive</span>.

<span class="source">Malle (2006), Psychological Bulletin 132(6)</span>

```notes
- Rough sense of scale: numbers you would struggle to see in a room this size.
- Nisbett is an author on the 1977 paper we just used and on the 1971 hypothesis we are now declining to use. That is what an argued literature looks like.
```

So we're not going to use it.

```notes
- The asleep test is ours. Its justification is the plain one: a rule about an exchange is testable next week, a verdict about a person is not.
- This slide is the course applied to itself. Check the claim that would have been most convenient for you, first.
```

## Except for one thing

The asymmetry **did** hold for **negative** events. It reversed for positive ones.

You write a field note when something **didn't go smoothly**.

The one condition where the effect appears is the condition your journal is written in.

```notes
- Sharper than the thing it replaced, and narrower. That trade is the whole method.
```

---

## The field note

1. **What happened**: one exchange, people by role
2. **The rule you inferred**: runnable by someone who wasn't there
3. **The test**: a second instance, failures included
4. **The rule now**: revised or held, and why

Describe the exchange **before** you interpret it.

## What double empathy does and doesn't say

**Does:** the misunderstanding is mutual, and reciprocity is the thing to look at.

**Doesn't:** that nobody has any difficulty. Milton relocates the problem; he doesn't dissolve it.

```notes
- Say this plainly, because the slogan version gets repeated without it and a student will meet the slogan version this week.
- It is a theoretical piece. The evidence arrives in week 11, and they should hold it lightly until then.
```

---

## What the thin-slice study doesn't say

It doesn't say autistic people are hard to talk to. It says **observers judged fast, on style, and then withdrew.**

Strip the audio-visual cues, judge the content alone, and the gap **went away**.

```notes
- The paper's own vocabulary is clinical; its finding points squarely at the observer. Cite the finding, not the vocabulary.
- For the fluent half of the room: this is a result about what you do in the first few seconds, and it is the most actionable thing in the week for you.
```

## One complete entry

**1. What happened.** Thursday lab, five of us. I asked the tutor a question; she answered the group. I asked again more specifically; she answered me directly.

**2. The rule.** A general question gets a general answer addressed to the room. A specific one gets answered to the asker.

**3. The test.** Tried it Friday in the stats tutorial. Held. The tutor answered me directly both times, and I had been specific both times.

**4. The rule now.** Held, narrowed: this is about how the question is framed, not about who asks it. Untested with a tutor who doesn't know the class.

```notes
- Point at the length of box 1 against box 4. Most of the writing is the exchange.
- Point at "untested with". Naming the limit is what makes it a specification rather than an opinion, and it is the clause first entries leave out.
```

## In the room: the asleep test, ten sentences (7 min)

Ten sentences, one at a time. Hands up: **does it survive being asleep?**

If it does, it's about the person. It fails.

```notes
- Three are deliberately borderline. When the room splits, stop and let them argue. That is the useful part.
- Do not resolve all three. Leaving one open is honest and it gets written up in somebody's journal.
```

## In the room: rewrite one of yours (8 min)

**Four minutes, alone.** One sentence: something you concluded about a person this week. It doesn't leave your notebook, so write the real one.

**Four minutes.** Rewrite it as a field note --- exchange, rule inferred, how you'd test it.

```notes
- Nobody reads these out. Say that before they start writing, or they will write a safe one.
- Collect one thing at the end: hands up if the rewrite gave you something you could actually check.
```

## What makes a rule runnable

| Not a rule | A rule |
| --- | --- |
| "People find him too intense." | "In this tutorial, a second follow-up on the same point gets answered to the group, not the asker." |
| "You have to read the room." | "When two people have agreed out loud, a third disagreement is raised as a question." |
| "Jess isn't reliable." | "Enthusiasm in the chat doesn't commit anyone; a commitment is a named person plus a date." |

Could someone who wasn't there run it tomorrow, and find out if it holds?

```notes
- The left column is where their first entries will land. Say so now, so that seeing it in their own writing is recognition rather than failure.
```
