# Payload and Envelope

Week 3. Indirect speech, and the spec that breaks itself on purpose.

```notes
- Open with the sentence, not the theory. Say it flatly and wait: "We should get coffee sometime."
- Ask who has replied "sure, Thursday at 10?" Don't make it a show of hands if the room is tense; just leave the pause.
```

---

## "We should get coffee sometime."

A request to schedule coffee?

If you've answered with a day and a time and watched the other face do something complicated, you know.

If you've never once wondered, you know too --- and you couldn't say how.

```notes
- Both halves of the room have the same information here and only one of them can state it. That asymmetry is the week.
```

---

## There is a published spec

Grice's Cooperative Principle, and four categories of maxim under it.

| Category | The maxim, in short |
| --- | --- |
| Quantity | as informative as required, no more |
| Quality | don't say what you believe false |
| Relation | be relevant |
| Manner | be perspicuous |

<span class="source">Grice (1975), Syntax and Semantics 3, 41–58</span>

```notes
- Read it as a spec and the protocol fails it constantly. "We should get coffee sometime" has no time, no place, no commitment.
```

---

## The author filed the bug himself

Alongside the maxims, Grice notes others --- "such as 'Be polite'" --- that also generate meaning beyond what is said.

And that stating the maxims as though talk existed to exchange information is "too narrow".

<MarginNote label="Known issue">The author of the specification filed the bug report in the same paragraph.</MarginNote>

```notes
- So: a published spec, plus a known exception the author flagged. Politeness is allowed to override the other four.
- Nobody told you which one wins. That's the documentation problem, exactly.
```

## Why anyone sends an envelope

- **Plausible deniability** --- a cooperative listener accepts, an unwilling one declines, and neither has to name a refusal
- Talk does **two jobs**: carries information, and negotiates the relationship
- An explicit statement becomes **common knowledge**; a hint never does

<span class="source">Pinker, Nowak & Lee (2008), Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 105(3)</span>

```notes
- The third one is why "we both knew" and "you said it" are different events under this protocol.
```

## The decoder

| Utterance | Maxim bent | Payload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We should get coffee sometime." | Quantity: no time, no place | I'd like us to be on friendly terms. |
| "No worries if not!" | Quality: there may well be worries | I'd like this, and here's a painless exit. |
| "Is it cold in here?" | Relation: temperature, asked of a person | Could the window be closed? |
| "Interesting idea." | Quantity: no content | I'm not convinced. |
| "Just circling back on this." | Quantity: no new information | This is late. |

These are the course's entries, not a research table. Test them in the lab.

```notes
- Take one column at a time down the table, not one row. The maxim column is the transferable part.
```

## Decoding from both ends

**If you decode by reading the words**: it's a lookup until it isn't. When the stakes matter, ask for the payload --- and take the blame for the ambiguity.

> "Do you mean coffee as a plan, or as a nice thing to say? Either's fine. I just want to know whether to put it in my calendar."

**If you decode without noticing**: your exercise runs the other way. Send one payload bare, and notice what it costs you.

```notes
- Say the script out loud, at speed, so they hear that it's sayable. It is four seconds long.
- Speak to both halves in turn, and don't rank them.
```

---

## Sending it bare is always valid

It also changes the relationship a little, because it creates common knowledge where a hint wouldn't.

That's a cost, not a mistake. Sometimes it's worth paying.

You can lower it by naming the choice: *"I'm going to be literal, because I'd rather not get this wrong."*

---

## Over-decoding

Sometimes "is it cold in here?" is a question about the temperature.

Treating every sentence as a hint is as nonconforming as treating none as one, and more tiring.

**The rule of thumb:** decode when a maxim is *visibly* bent and you're plainly expected to do something. Otherwise take the words at face value --- and let them send a second, clearer envelope.

```notes
- Under this protocol, they will. That's the reassuring half: the sender retries.
```
