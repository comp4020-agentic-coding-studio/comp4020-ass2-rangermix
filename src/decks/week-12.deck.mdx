## Why three things are out of scope

Each one runs on top of **everything** the course documented --- handshakes, envelopes, timing, repair, masking, disclosure, rate limits --- all at once.

Each is high-stakes enough that a first-year course would teach it badly in one week.

And in each, running the protocol *on purpose* raises questions this course can't settle for you.

```notes
- That's the honest reason, and it's why this week sketches rather than teaches. Say it as a limitation, not as modesty.
```

## OI-1: Romance

**Status:** open. **Out of scope because:** every earlier week at once, with the highest cost of error.

Five styles of communicating romantic interest --- traditional, physical, sincere, playful, polite --- from over five thousand adults. Physical, sincere and playful correlated with more dating success.

<span class="source">Hall et al. (2010), Communication Quarterly 58(4)</span>

```notes
- The inventory measures how people say they signal interest, not what receivers understand. That gap matters.
- The course's inference, not the paper's: if there are five styles, "I can't tell when someone's flirting" is sometimes a style mismatch. Week 3's decoder handles sincere well and playful badly.
```

## OI-2: Persuasion

**Status:** open. **Out of scope because:** the protocol used deliberately to move someone, where the ethics are the hard part.

Research on compliance organised around three goals people already pursue: seeing things **accurately**, keeping their **relationships**, feeling good about **themselves**.

Much of the influence described is subtle, indirect, and outside the target's awareness.

<span class="source">Cialdini & Goldstein (2004), Annual Review of Psychology 55</span>

<MarginNote label="Security note">A protocol that works outside awareness is, by definition, an attack surface.</MarginNote>

```notes
- Everything in weeks 1 to 11 can be used to persuade. This course taught it so they could interoperate.
- That's why the Field Experiment's ethics reflection is graded, not an appendix.
```

---

## OI-3: Power

**Status:** open, and **contested**. **Out of scope because:** it changes every other parameter, and the research is still arguing.

Elevated power activates **approach**: more positive affect, more automatic processing, more disinhibited behaviour.

Reduced power activates **inhibition**: more attention to threat and to **other people's interests**, more controlled processing.

<span class="source">Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson (2003), Psychological Review 110(2)</span>

```notes
- The account has drawn published criticism. Treat it as a proposal, not a settled result, and say so.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

If that proposal holds even partly: whoever holds less power runs more of the protocol.

```notes
- They attend more to the other person, inhibit more, and pay week 6's costs more often.
- Week 11's finding that conversion falls on the minority is the same shape. The connection is the course's; the proposal is theirs.
```
