## Diffusion chains

Eight people. The first hears a story and retells it to the second, who retells it to the third, down the chain.

Some chains all autistic. Some all non-autistic. Some mixed.

<span class="source">Crompton, Ropar et al. (2020), Autism 24(7)</span>

## Where the loss happens

| Chain | Detail retained | Rapport |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All autistic | no significant difference from non-autistic chains | higher than mixed |
| All non-autistic | no significant difference from autistic chains | higher than mixed |
| Mixed | **declined significantly more steeply** | lower |

<MarginNote label="Root cause">Packet loss at the boundary between two implementations. Neither endpoint is faulty.</MarginNote>

```notes
- Read it as an engineer would: information survived within either implementation and degraded fastest across them.
- And the decisive bit: if autistic people simply lacked the ability to share information, the all-autistic chains would have done worst. They didn't.
```

## Misreading runs both ways

People filmed reacting to events; non-autistic observers guessed which event.

Observers were **less accurate for autistic people** --- yet autistic people were rated **just as expressive** for most events, and their reactions were described differently.

<span class="source">Sheppard et al. (2016), Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 46(4)</span>

```notes
- Their reactions differed. They were not reduced. That distinction is the week in miniature.
```

---

## Same judgement, different consequence

Ask autistic observers too: autistic and non-autistic raters made **similar** trait judgements of autistic adults.

The difference was what came next --- for autistic raters, those judgements **didn't reduce their interest in interacting**.

<span class="source">DeBrabander et al. (2019), Autism in Adulthood 1(4)</span>

```notes
- So both implementations notice the difference. What varies is whether the difference counts against someone.
```

## Score the videos for readability

Sheppard's videoed targets, scored for **how readable** they were. New neurotypical perceivers rated them.

Neurotypical targets rated more favourably --- whether the greeting's details were **concealed** (Study 1) or **disclosed** (Study 2).

<span class="source">Alkhaldi, Sheppard & Mitchell (2019), JADD 49(10)</span>

```notes
- The concealed/disclosed manipulation matters: it rules out the easy benign explanation that perceivers were just confused about the situation.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Readability correlated with favourability --- r = .58 and r = .63 --- independent of target diagnosis.

<MarginNote label="Two parties">A property of a reading, not of a face.</MarginNote>

```notes
- Read the last clause twice. The judgement is not attached to a category: a hard-to-read neurotypical target got the same treatment.
- That is the double empathy problem with a correlation coefficient on it.
- And the authors' own hedge, which we keep: other interpretations of the correlation are also possible. It stays correlational.
```

## In the room: readable to whom? (7 min)

**Three minutes.** One person you find easy to read, one you find hard. Write one concrete **behaviour** each. The asleep test applies.

**Three minutes, in pairs.** Swap. Would your partner's "easy" behaviours be easy for *you*?

**One minute.** Hands up if anything on their easy list was on your hard list.

```notes
- Whatever the count, it is a finding about this room and no other. Say that.
- The point of any disagreement: "hard to read" needs a second half. Hard to read by whom.
```

## Rapport signals are implementation-specific

Back to the diffusion-chain videos, coding **mutual gaze** and **backchannelling** --- the "mm-hm", the nod.

In mixed pairs, less of both went with lower rapport.

In autistic pairs, **less backchannelling went with higher rapport**.

<span class="source">Rifai et al. (2022), Autism in Adulthood 4(1)</span>

```notes
- So the visible signs that someone is engaged are part of an implementation, not a universal readout.
- A listener who never says "mm-hm" may be the most engaged person in the room. One who says it constantly may be running a week 6 subroutine.
```

## Five minutes with a stranger

Not clips. Not vignettes. **125 people**, put into pairs --- both autistic, both non-autistic, or mixed.

Five minutes of unstructured conversation with someone they'd never met, then both rate it.

<span class="source">Morrison et al. (2020), Autism 24(5)</span>

```notes
- Everything up to here has been ratings of recordings. This is the question run as an actual conversation, with both people's judgements collected.
```

---

## The bleak half

Autistic adults were rated <span data-quoted>more awkward, less attractive, and less socially warm</span> ---

by autistic **and** non-autistic partners alike.

```notes
- Do not soften this and do not rush past it. The judgement travelled across both groups.
- Sit with it for a second before the next slide, because the next slide is the whole week.
```

---

{/* _class: impact */}

Only the non-autistic adults' interest in meeting again followed that judgement.

```notes
- Autistic participants trended the other way -- towards preferring other autistic partners.
- So the rating and the withdrawal come apart. One is shared; the other is not. Whatever "awkward" measures, it is not what decides whether somebody wants to see you again.
```

---

## And the number that matters most

Autistic participants reported **disclosing more about themselves** to autistic than to non-autistic partners.

**How much a person opens up is not a fixed property of that person.** It moved with who they sat opposite --- in five minutes, with a stranger.

```notes
- They have met this claim before, from a different field: week 9, Kluger and Malloy, a third of the variance in question-asking belonging to the pair rather than the person.
- Two methods, two literatures, one shape. This is the strongest argument in the course for interoperation over conversion.
```

```notes

```

## In the room: the pairing effect (8 min)

**Four minutes.** One thing about how you talk that changes depending on who you're with. Then the hard half: **how would you know if you were wrong?** What would you observe?

**Three minutes, in pairs.** Read out only the *test*. Check each other's tests are runnable --- week 2's rule, at the last week of content.

**One minute.** Anyone whose test needs the other person's cooperation: say so.

```notes
- That last minute is the week's point, and it is the first clause of their bilateral protocol.
```

---

## Who pays for the mismatch

12 autistic adults on time with neurotypical friends and family: being in a minority, and **conforming to what the other people were used to** in order to spend time with them.

With autistic people: belonging, and being able to be themselves.

<span class="source">Crompton, Hallett et al. (2020), Autism 24(6)</span>

Over years, the majority's misreadings push towards isolation --- which damages mental health, and costs everyone else what those people would have contributed.

<span class="source">Mitchell, Sheppard & Cassidy (2021), British Journal of Developmental Psychology 39(1)</span>

---

{/* _class: impact */}

The loss happens between two implementations.

So the fix is bilateral.

## A clause that won't work

| Weak clause | Why | Fixed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Alex will be more direct." | no scope, no check, no end | "Alex puts requests in the doc as questions." |
| "Sam will try to contribute more." | a verdict wearing a clause | "Sam says 'sounds good' out loud when agreeing." |
| "We'll communicate better." | nobody conceded anything | pick one behaviour each, and a way to check it |

The test is week 2's: could someone who wasn't there run it, and find out whether it held?

```notes
- The left column is where their first drafts will land on Thursday, so show it now rather than marking it later.
- "Try to" in a clause means nobody will know whether it happened.
```

## Conversion against interoperation

| Question | Conversion | Interoperation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who changes | one side, entirely | both sides, partly |
| Scope | everywhere, indefinitely | this exchange, this relationship |
| When it breaks | silently, blamed on oneself | out loud, with an agreed signal |
| What it costs | the week 6 ledger, for one person | a few clauses each |

```notes
- Conversion is the default, and the pressure to convert falls on the minority --- week 6's pressure column.
- It goes the other way too: telling a colleague to "just be direct" and expecting them to rebuild their implementation for you.
```

## The bilateral protocol

Alex backchannels constantly, hints, answers fast. Sam answers slowly, speaks directly, rarely says "mm-hm".

| # | Clause | Who concedes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sam can answer by message within 24 hours, not on the spot | Alex: immediacy |
| 2 | Alex puts requests in writing, stated directly | Alex: the envelope |
| 3 | Silence isn't disagreement; either can say "still thinking" | both: the timeout |
| 4 | Sam says "sounds good" out loud when agreeing | Sam: one token |
| 5 | Either can say **"mismatch"**, with no explanation owed | both |

```notes
- Clause 5 makes the others safe. It turns a mismatch from something one person privately blames themselves for into a word both agreed to say.
- Every clause has a check in the report: did any question go unanswered past a day, did either of us read a silence as a no.
```

## This room is the worked example

Eleven weeks. A deliberately mixed class, building vocabulary:

*payload* · *timeout* · *repair initiator* · *rate limit* · *ledger* · *mismatch*

Not anyone's native implementation. A shared protocol, negotiated one week at a time.

```notes
- It lets people who run the majority protocol fluently and people who never got it handed to them talk about the same exchange in the same terms.
- That was the argument all along. Point at the glossary: it's the record.
```

## In the room: one clause each (9 min)

Your Interoperation Report partner is in this room.

**Three minutes.** Separately: one thing the other does that costs you something. A **behaviour**, not a verdict.

**Four minutes.** Together: turn both into one clause --- what each side will do, and how either checks it next week.

**Two minutes.** Agree your mismatch word.

```notes
- One clause is a real protocol. Five is Thursday's job. Do not let them try to write the whole document here.
```

## What interoperation is not

- **Not meeting in the middle.** Clauses needn't be symmetrical. One side may concede four things and the other one.
- **Not permanent.** Scoped to a relationship and a situation, and renegotiable --- saying so is what makes it safe to agree now.
- **Not a promise that it works.** Every clause is a hypothesis; clause 5 exists because some will be wrong.

```notes
- The term arrives late and has to carry the whole course, so spend a minute on what it is not.
- A protocol with no way to report a fault is week 1's problem all over again.
```

## What this week added to the vocabulary

- **interoperation** --- two implementations agreeing a protocol for the exchange at hand
- **conversion** --- one implementation replacing itself
- the **bilateral protocol** --- the clauses, and the mismatch word
